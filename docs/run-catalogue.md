### 3. A Pu.1⁺-density threshold detector finds ~95 % of manual cores but produces 3.7–5× the manual core area and calls lesions in control sections
*Notebook:* `notebooks/04_manual_vs_automatic.ipynb` · *Code:* `lesionseg/lesion.py`, `lesionseg/density.py`, `lesionseg/validate.py` · *Results:* `results/lesion_threshold_grid.csv`, `results/lesion_calibration_table.csv`

**Rationale.** Threshold baseline for comparison. The first lesion detector (scaffold, commit e28d909) scored lesions as a robust z-score of smoothed Pu.1⁺ cells/mm² with a myeloid gate against hypercellular non-myeloid regions (central canal ependyma, grey matter, meninges). The grid calibrates its parameters against the manual CORE polygons and serves as the baseline for the supervised model (§4).

**Technical approach.** Density grid of 10 µm bins smoothed with σ 40 µm; score = robust z of Pu.1⁺ density relative to all tissue bins; hysteresis threshold (`low` z, optional `seed` z) and minimum lesion area. Grid of 40 settings: low 2.5–4.0, seed none or 5–10, min area 5,000–15,000 µm². Metrics: `det_frac` = manual cores detected (> 50 % inside), `ctrl_area_mm2` = lesion area in CFA / OS control sections, `excess_frac` = automatic area in annotated sections outside manual core + 100 µm. The calibration table classifies 215 detected lesions as TP(core) (61), FP-in-annotated-section (93) or FP-control-section (61) with area, Pu.1⁺/nuclear density, Pu.1⁺ fraction, score and relative radius.

**Results.** Best settings: low 3.0 (no seed, 5,000 µm²) det_frac 0.955, control area 0.140 mm², excess 0.38, 10.43 mm² lesion area in annotated sections, 190 lesions; low 2.5: 0.955 / 0.206 mm² / 0.43 / 12.31 mm²; low 3.5 + seed 5: 0.946 / 0.087 mm² / 0.32 / 9.00 mm². Across the grid det_frac 0.72–0.955, control area 0.027–0.206 mm², excess 0.25–0.43. Manual cores total 2.46 mm² over the 4 scenes (§7), so the best settings outline ~3.7–5× that area. Calibration table medians: TP lesions 82,700 µm², score 5.94; FPs in annotated sections 14,400 µm², score 3.37; FPs in control sections 22,500 µm², score 3.92. REPORT.md: the threshold detector's excess includes the central-canal region and grey matter.

**Caveats.** No script in the repo regenerates lesion_threshold_grid.csv or lesion_calibration_table.csv; `scripts/publish_results.py` only copies them from `outputs/`, and they date from commit f933038 (2026-09-29). The calibration table's detector settings are not recorded in the file.

### 4. A gradient-boosting lesion model trained on manual cores, scored leave-one-scene-out, reaches average precision 0.68–0.92 and detects 79–95 % of held-out manual cores
*Notebook:* `notebooks/04_manual_vs_automatic.ipynb` · *Code:* `lesionseg/model.py`, `scripts/train_lesion_model.py`, `configs/eae_dvp_sdata.yaml` · *Results:* `results/model_training_report.csv`

**Rationale.** Model: leave-one-scene-out training report. The threshold detector (§3) over-called (canal, grey matter, control sections). A supervised model learns "lesion" per grid bin from the collaborator's manual CORE polygons, while every scene is scored by a model that never saw its own annotations (commit f933038, 2026-09-29).

**Technical approach.** Unit: 10 µm density-grid bin. 19 features from the cell table: nuclear density, Pu.1⁺ density and Pu.1⁺ fraction at 20 / 40 / 80 µm smoothing; robust z-scores of the 40 µm maps (per scene); smoothed Pu.1 and Iba1 intensity of Pu.1⁺ and all cells (40 µm); relative radial position (0 centre ≈ canal, 1 pia), distance to the section edge relative and in µm. Labels: 1 inside CORE polygons; 0 in tissue ≥ 200 µm from any core in annotated sections and in all tissue of control sections; the band in between ignored. Negatives subsampled to ≤ 10 per positive. `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` (300 iterations, learning rate 0.06, 31 leaves, min 50 per leaf, L2 1.0, balanced class weights, seed 0). Leave-one-scene-out over 4 scenes, trained on run `outputs/sdata_unrestricted`; plus a model on all scenes. In the pipeline the probability map is thresholded at p > 0.25 grown from p > 0.5 seeds, min area 5,000 µm², smoothed 20 µm, myeloid gate off; each scene uses its own held-out model. Metrics on the held-out scene: bin-level ROC AUC and average precision, and fraction of manual cores > 50 % inside p > 0.25.

**Results.** Held-out CML_1: AUC 0.996, AP 0.923, 34 cores, 0.794 detected; CML_2_rescan: 0.995 / 0.903 / 25 / 0.920; CML_metal s0: 0.996 / 0.839 / 33 / 0.818; CML_metal s1: 0.995 / 0.679 / 20 / 0.950.

**Reading.** Bin-level AUC is near 1 because most tissue is trivially negative; average precision and cores detected are the informative columns (notebook 04).

**Caveats.** Only 4 scenes (two of them two scenes of one slide); a fold is one scene, so between-animal generalisation is not separately tested. The training-report detection rates (p > 0.25 alone) are higher than the final pipeline's core coverage (0.64–0.90, §7), which adds seeding, minimum area, smoothing and, later, clipping to parenchyma. Labels are cores only; the rim is never labelled.

### 6. Lesion zones scale with section size (rim ≈ 5 %, peri and deep ≈ 15 % of the section radius) and the Pu.1⁺ fraction falls from core 0.47 and rim 0.43 to peri / deep 0.30 and distal 0.23
*Notebook:* `notebooks/03_zones_relative_distance.ipynb` · *Code:* `lesionseg/lesion.py`, `lesionseg/assign.py`, `lesionseg/export.py` · *Results:* `results/CML_1/scene0/` · *Figure:* `results/CML_1/scene0/distance_profile.png`, `results/CML_1/scene0/zone_polygons.png`, `results/CML_1/scene0/section_cells_P2_3_C.png`

**Rationale.** Zones and relative distances: sections differ in size (cervical vs thoracic vs lumbar), so fixed µm widths would mean different things in different sections. Zone widths and capture rings are therefore fractions of each section's equivalent radius, and each cell carries a relative radial position and a relative signed distance to the lesion edge (commit f933038). The deep zone, a further band beyond peri, was added the same day (commit 891018d) to implement the collaborator's "additional step" after the peri-lesion area (`additional-notes.md`); zone polygons with holes and section attribution were exported for region LMD (commits f4d4776, 2115ceb, 19351b4).

**Technical approach.** Equivalent radius R = √(area/π) per section. Zones: core = inside lesion deeper than 0.05 R; rim = inside within 0.05 R of the edge; peri = outside within 0.15 R; deep = a further 0.15 R; distal = beyond, and all tissue of control sections. Every cell gets `zone`, signed `dist_to_lesion_um` (negative inside), `dist_to_lesion_rel`, `rel_pos` (0 centre ≈ canal, 1 pia) and `dist_bin` (−100 … 300 µm). Notebook sections: section sizes and the zone widths that resulted; Pu.1⁺ fraction versus relative distance to the lesion edge per lesion section; where the lesions sit (radial position of Pu.1⁺ cells by zone); zone composition of Pu.1⁺ cells per section; Pu.1 and Iba1 intensity by zone. Zone polygons (core / rim / peri / deep, dense non-myeloid) go to GeoJSON and a WKT table for QuPath and `lesionseg export-zones-lmd`.

**Results.** Section radii 726–1,234 µm for full sections (R1_2_C fragments 277–331 µm), giving rim 36–62 µm and peri 107–185 µm (R1_2_C 14–17 / 42–50 µm). Pu.1⁺ fraction by zone, 4 scenes pooled (zone_summary.csv): core 0.467 (10,353 / 22,157), rim 0.430 (7,929 / 18,443), peri 0.300, deep 0.302, distal 0.225 (includes control sections), meninges 0.299. CML_1 distance profile (25 µm bins): Pu.1⁺ fraction 0.41–0.51 from −187 to −37 µm, 0.35 at +12.5 µm, 0.30–0.34 from +62 µm outward.

**Caveats.** Rim and core are geometric bands of the automatic lesion, not biologically defined. Distal mixes lesion-section tissue with control sections. Intensity-by-zone panels are figures only here; their quantitative follow-up is in notebooks 07–08 (not part of this slice).

Per section: outer layer − parenchyma 20–80 µm median ΔS +0.39 (bootstrap CI 0.37–0.57), 17/17 sections, 6 animals; outer − deeper meninges +0.12, 10/12 sections (Wilcoxon p = 0.005); outer − parenchyma 150–300 µm +0.69, 17/17.

**Reading.** The outer meningeal layer is a sheet of nuclei lying along the pia – what flattened lining cells look like – and not an edge artefact (parenchyma right under the same surface is only weakly aligned).

**Caveats.** Shape and position only; no identity marker. The surface direction is ill-defined in narrow strands and flaps.

### 32. Alignment with the surface fades within ~100 µm of depth; deeper parenchyma is slightly radial
*Notebook:* `notebooks/12_nuclear_orientation.ipynb` · *Code:* `lesionseg/orientation.py` (depth_alignment) · *Results:* `results/notebooks/12_nuclear_orientation.ipynb`

**Rationale.** "Alignment against depth below the surface": does the parallel orientation stop at the meninges or follow the tissue in? No compartment boundary is involved here.

**Technical approach.** Order parameter S of elongated nuclei (any compartment) per depth bin (0–10 … 300–500 µm) per slide, bins with ≥ 30 nuclei.

**Results.** All four slides: S ≈ 0.54–0.64 at 0–10 µm, ≈ 0.2 at 40–60 µm, crossing 0 at ~80–100 µm, ≈ −0.1 to −0.2 at 120–300 µm (read from the figure; the pooled 150–300 µm group in §31 is S = −0.129, beyond its shuffle null −0.036 to −0.001).

**Reading.** The surface alignment is a surface property. The slight radial preference deeper in could reflect radial glial / astrocytic septa of the white matter; not followed up.

**Caveats.** Per-bin values are only in the figure.

### 33. The surface alignment survives every robustness check and is visible in random windows
*Notebook:* `notebooks/12_nuclear_orientation.ipynb` · *Code:* `lesionseg/orientation.py` (section_alignment, paired_test) · *Results:* `results/notebooks/12_nuclear_orientation.ipynb`

**Rationale.** "Robustness: outer meninges vs the edge control under other choices" and "Random examples": the result should not hinge on the eccentricity cut-off, the surface smoothing, segmentation quality, the Pu.1 status or lesion vs control sections.

**Technical approach.** Recompute per-section S of the outer layer and of parenchyma 20–80 µm and the paired difference for: eccentricity ≥ 0.8 / 0.9; surface smoothing σ = 2 / 10 / 20 µm; solidity ≥ 0.9; Pu.1⁻ only; Pu.1⁺ only (≥ 5 nuclei); lesion sections only; control sections only. Random 140 µm windows centred on random outer-layer nuclei (seed 1, ≥ 8 of them per window, ≥ 300 µm apart), every elongated nucleus drawn as a line coloured by its surface angle.

**Results.** Median ΔS (sections higher): default 0.39 (17/17); ecc ≥ 0.8 0.37 (17/17); ecc ≥ 0.9 0.39 (15/15); σ = 2 µm 0.28 (17/17); σ = 10 µm 0.45; σ = 20 µm 0.46; solidity ≥ 0.9 0.38 (16/16); Pu.1⁻ 0.39 (17/17); Pu.1⁺ 0.37 (11/12, outer-layer S = 0.65); lesion sections 0.37 (8/8); control sections 0.54 (9/9). In the random windows, thin surface layers are uniformly parallel; poorly aligned windows are narrow strands or flaps.

**Reading.** Elongated Pu.1⁺ nuclei of the outer layer are aligned just as strongly – flattened macrophages along the surface rather than cells migrating inward.

**Caveats.** Pu.1⁺ check has fewer sections (12).

### 34. Nuclear shape alone predicts the rule's meninges only weakly (AUC 0.72); good scores need edge geometry, which just relearns the rule
*Code:* `lesionseg/meninges_model.py` · *Results:* `STATUS.md`

**Rationale.** Can a model find the meninges from nuclear elongation? The only labels available are those of the adaptive boundary (§13), so this was run as a feasibility check.

**Technical approach.** Cells in sections, label 1 = surface meninges ≤ 80 µm deep, 0 = parenchyma (≈ 202k cells, 11 % meninges). Features per cell: own eccentricity, area, solidity; neighbours' elongated share, mean eccentricity and axis alignment at 15 / 30 / 60 µm; then density and Pu.1; then one-sidedness (offset of the neighbours' centroid, an edge signal). Gradient boosting, leave-one-animal-out.

**Results.** Shape only: AUC 0.72, average precision 0.26 (base rate 0.11). + density, Pu.1: 0.76 / 0.31. + one-sidedness: 0.87 / 0.53. One-sidedness + density without any shape: 0.87 / 0.53.

**Reading.** Elongation is a real but weak cell-level signal (only ~31 % of outer-layer nuclei are elongated, swollen meninges are round). The high scores come from geometry and only reproduce how the rule defines meninges – real labels are the bottleneck.

**Caveats.** Exploratory script, not kept in the repo; numbers recorded in STATUS.md.

### 35. Meninges annotation targets and a training / evaluation setup are ready: 8 sections, every animal, 31 boxes
*Code:* `lesionseg/meninges_targets.py`, `lesionseg/meninges_model.py`, `scripts/meninges_annotation_targets.py`, `scripts/train_meninges_model.py` · *Results:* `results/meninges_annotation/` · *Docs:* `docs/meninges_annotation.md`

**Rationale.** Following §34: get independent meninges labels where they teach the most, and have the evaluation ready when they arrive.

**Technical approach.** Sections: every animal first, then by swollen segments, lesion cores at the surface and lesion status; one replicate slide per section, balancing the slides unless one replicate is clearly more intact. Per section up to five 300 µm boxes: core_at_surface (manual core ≤ 50 µm from the surface), swollen (highest meningeal cellularity), internal (densest meninges > 80 µm deep), bare (≤ 1 meningeal nucleus per segment: tear or thin pia), typical (median cellularity). Previews show the raw image only. Annotation layers MENINGES_ROI / MENINGES / MENINGES_UNSURE (napari → GeoJSON). Training: labels 1 inside MENINGES, 0 inside a box otherwise; features in groups shape / markers / density / image / geometry; leave-one-animal-out; the rule scored against the same labels, overall and per box kind.

**Results.** 8 sections (R1_2_T, P3_1_C, P2_3_T, R1_3_T, OS1_2_L, CFA_L2_L, P3_1_T, P2_3_L) on all 4 slides; 31 boxes: 6 core_at_surface, 8 swollen, 1 internal, 8 bare, 8 typical. Dry run on rule-derived fake labels: ~7k labelled cells, full chain works.

**Caveats.** Infrastructure only; no real annotations yet. Only one internal box (deep meninges are rare in these sections).
